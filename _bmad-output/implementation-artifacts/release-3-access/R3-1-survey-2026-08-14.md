# R3-1: the survey. Which server routes let the wrong office desk through

**Date:** 2026-08-14. **Read-only. Nothing was changed, nothing was widened, nothing was
deployed. No live school data was read.**

This answers the question the plan said was being guessed at: how big is the gap between
the written-down permission table and what the server actually allows.

**The short answer: bigger than the one bad example suggested. Of the 100 routes checked,
63 let all eight office desks through, and 62 of those 63 are wrong against the table.
41 of the 63 are writes.**

---

## How this was done

Two things, in order.

1. **Counted.** Every route handler in the backend was listed with its gate. There are
   **484 route handlers**. **153** of them are gated by role alone in a way that lets
   every admin desk pass: `require_role(...)` with `"admin"` in the list. Of those, **101**
   use the narrowest form, `require_role("owner", "admin")`, which is what R3-1 was asked
   to survey. The other **52** use a wider list (usually adding `"teacher"`) and carry the
   same flaw for office desks; they are a second tier and are listed at the end.

2. **Probed, not read.** Each of the 101 routes was called eight times, once as each
   office desk: principal, accountant head, management head, transport head, front desk,
   IT, maintenance, support staff. The authorisation code exercised is the same code
   production runs. One route was skipped because it is a live stream that never returns
   (`GET /api/attendance/stream`), so 100 were measured.

Reading alone would have been wrong in both directions. Some routes with a wide-looking
gate are narrowed further down, inside the handler or inside the service underneath, and
a reader would have reported a hole that is not there. Others look narrow and are not.

**How a result was counted.** Anything other than "refused" counts as passing the gate.
Where an answer was a complaint about the shape of the request rather than a refusal, the
gate had already passed. For nine of the highest-stakes routes the probe was repeated with
a properly shaped request so that a complaint could never be mistaken for a refusal.

## The three findings that matter most

### 1. Transport is the one place the table says an explicit NO and the server says yes

Thirteen transport routes let all eight desks through, including creating, changing and
deleting a bus route.

The management head is not merely unlisted for transport. Decision 2 of 10 August named
five transport tools and **denied** them to him in writing, so that the accountant head
would run transport until the transport head's release. The server hands him all thirteen
routes anyway. This is the only case found where a written refusal is contradicted rather
than simply not enforced, which makes it the clearest one to fix first.

The same thirteen are open to the front desk, IT, maintenance and support staff, none of
whom has a transport screen.

### 2. Two routes are open to everybody that the table gives to almost nobody

- **`POST /api/settings/year-end-transition`** moves the school into a new academic year
  and promotes the roll. The table treats the year-end promotion as the school owner's
  alone, the principal expressly included in the exclusion. Every office desk passes this
  gate, support staff included.
- **`GET /api/settings/branches`** reads the school's branch records, also owner-only in
  the table. Every desk gets a 200 back.

### 3. The certificate route is the one R2-9 was built to control, and it was missed

`POST /api/ops/certificates` creates a certificate and returned **200 for all eight
desks**, proven with a real request body, not inferred.

Its sibling, the ID card request route, is correctly narrowed to the owner, principal,
management head and accountant head, and carries a written comment saying it is
deliberately not `require_role("admin", "owner")` because that would hand a write route to
the five dormant profiles. **Somebody saw this exact problem, fixed one route and left the
one beside it.** That is worth noting because it says the fault is not ignorance; it is
that there is no single place to make the decision, so it has to be remembered route by
route.

## Every one of the 63, with a judgement

"Should all eight pass?" is judged against `profile_matrix.py`, which is the school's
written-down statement of who holds what.

### Transport, 13 routes. NO.

`GET/POST /api/ops/transport`, `PATCH` and `DELETE /api/ops/transport/{route_id}`,
`GET /api/ops/transport/roster`, `GET/POST /api/ops/transport/vehicles`,
`POST /api/ops/transport/zones`,
`PATCH /api/ops/transport/students/{student_id}/coordinates`,
`PATCH /api/ops/transport/zones/{zone_id}/centroid`,
`POST /api/ops/transport/geocode`, `GET /api/ops/transport/suggest-route`,
`GET /api/ops/transport/cluster-analysis`.

Should pass: owner, principal, accountant head. The transport head is dormant and holds no
write tool until his release. **The management head is explicitly denied and passes anyway.**
Front desk, IT, maintenance and support staff should all be refused.

### Certificates, 1 route. NO.

`POST /api/ops/certificates`. Should pass: owner, principal, and the management and
accountant heads as a request awaiting approval. The five dormant desks have no
certificate screen. Proven at 200 for all eight.

### School-wide settings, 2 routes. NO.

`POST /api/settings/year-end-transition` and `GET /api/settings/branches`. Owner only.

### Messaging families, 5 routes. NO.

`POST /api/sms/send-reminder`, `POST /api/sms/send-bulk`,
`POST /api/sms/send-parent-message`, `GET /api/sms/logs`, `GET /api/sms/config-status`.

Should pass: owner, principal, management head for the parent message, accountant head for
the fee reminder, front desk once her release lands. A support staff account can currently
message the whole school's families.

### Announcements, 2 routes. NO.

`POST /api/ops/announcements`, `DELETE /api/ops/announcements/{ann_id}`. The announcement
screen belongs to the management head. Proven at 200 for all eight on the create.

### Timetable and substitutions, 5 routes. NO.

`POST /api/academics/timetable`, `PATCH` and `DELETE .../timetable/{slot_id}`,
`PUT /api/academics/timetable/import`, `POST /api/academics/substitutions`. The timetable
screen belongs to the management head and the two leaders. The accountant head and all
five dormant desks should be refused.

### Admissions, 6 routes. NO.

`GET /api/admissions/applications`, `GET .../{application_id}`,
`POST /api/admissions/applications`, `PATCH .../status`, `POST .../documents`,
`POST .../assessment`. The admissions screen belongs to the management head. Note the two
routes beside these, the offer and the enrolment, **are** correctly narrowed to the
principal, which again shows the decision being made in some places and not others.

### Activities and houses, 6 routes. NO.

`POST /api/activities/houses/{house_id}/points`, `POST` and
`DELETE /api/activities/positions...`, `POST /api/activities/teams`, `PATCH` and
`DELETE .../teams/{team_id}`. Management head's screen.

### Incidents, 4 routes. NO.

`GET /api/ops/incidents`, `GET .../{incident_id}`, `POST .../thread`, `PATCH .../assign`.
Should pass: owner, principal, management head, and the front desk once her release lands.
Not the accountant head, transport head, IT, maintenance or support staff.

### Enquiries and complaints, 5 routes. NO.

`GET/POST /api/ops/enquiries`, `PATCH .../{enquiry_id}`, `GET/POST /api/ops/complaints`.
The enquiry register belongs to the management head and the front desk.

### Assets, 4 routes. NO.

`GET/POST /api/ops/assets`, `PATCH` and `DELETE /api/ops/assets/{asset_id}`. The asset
screen belongs to the management head, the transport head and the front desk, and the last
two hold no write tool while dormant. The accountant head, IT, maintenance and support
staff should be refused.

### Attendance, 4 routes measured plus 1 unmeasured. NO.

`POST /api/attendance/staff/bulk` (proven at 200 for all eight, this one writes the
register), `GET /api/attendance/low-attendance`, `GET /api/attendance/export`,
`GET /api/attendance/staff`.

The accountant head holds attendance **read only** by decision 2, with the marking tools
deliberately absent, so his passing the bulk-write route is wrong on its own terms. The
five dormant desks should be refused all four. **`GET /api/attendance/stream` carries the
same gate and was not measured** because it is a live stream; treat it as the same answer
until somebody checks it.

### Staff leave, 2 routes. NO for the dormant five.

`GET /api/staff/{staff_id}/leave-requests`, `GET /api/staff/leaves/pending`. Correct for
the accountant and management heads, who hold the staff leave screen. Wrong for the five
dormant desks. **Both returned 200 against an empty stand-in database, so this survey
proves the gate passed and says nothing about whose leave records would come back on the
live system.** Worth checking separately.

### Campus reads, 2 routes. NO, and inconsistent with their own siblings.

`GET /api/campus/asset-custody`, `GET /api/campus/inventory/items`. Every write in the
same file is narrowed to a named list of desks. These two reads of the same records were
left open. Reading the stock list is a smaller matter than changing it, but the
inconsistency is unintended rather than decided.

### Notifications, 1 route. QUESTION, not a clear no.

`POST /api/notifications` sends a notification to a named user. There is no screen in the
table that corresponds to it, so the table cannot answer. It needs a decision rather than
a fix.

### The one that is fine after all

`POST /api/auth/admin/students/{student_id}/login`. The gate is wide, but the service
underneath refuses anyone who is not the owner, the principal or the management head. The
five dormant desks and the accountant head all got a plain refusal. **Correct today, but
correct by accident of where the check sits**, so it should not be taken as a pattern.

## The structural finding, which matters more than any single route

Where somebody did narrow a route, they wrote a fresh list of desk names on the spot.
Counting them: the accounting file has its own, the campus file has one helper called with
**six different lists**, operations has a front-desk list and an accounting list, issues
has an IT list, audit has a reader list, settings has an IT list for token limits, student
leave has an inline principal check, and admissions has one for the offer and the
enrolment.

That is roughly **a dozen separate hand-written statements of who may do what**, none of
which reads the written-down table, none of which knows about the others, and none of
which moves when the table moves.

It is exactly the fault Release 2 removed from the menus, one layer down. The menus used
to grant by subtraction; the server grants by whatever the person writing the route
happened to type.

**One concrete symptom.** Four library routes are narrowed to `{"principal", "librarian"}`.
There is no such thing as a librarian on this platform: the eight recognised office desks
do not include one. So the word does nothing, the four routes are principal-only, and
anybody reading the code would reasonably believe a librarian has access.

## The second tier: 52 more routes, not individually judged

These carry a wider gate, usually `require_role("owner", "admin", "teacher")`. They were
counted but not probed desk by desk, because R3-1 was scoped to the narrow form. **They
have the same hole for office desks**: adding "teacher" to the list does not make the
admin half of it any narrower.

They are mostly the teacher-facing academic screens: assignments, lesson plans, question
papers, worksheets, curriculum, PTM notes, quizzes, exam sheets, attendance correction,
resource bookings, library reads, and the two fee-status lookups. Four settings and class
lists are read-only reference data and probably harmless.

Two things stand out and should be checked when this tier is done:

- `GET /api/fees/status/{student_id}` and `GET /api/fees/discounts/{student_id}` are
  reachable by every admin desk. Money reads are otherwise sound, so these two deserve a
  direct look.
- `POST /api/attendance/student/bulk` writes a child's attendance register and is open to
  every desk.

## What I recommend, and what I did not do

**Nothing was changed.** Every route above is exactly as it was.

The instinct is to go and narrow 62 gates. I would not. Sixty-two hand-edits is how an
access accident happens, and it would leave the thirteenth list of desk names in the
codebase rather than removing the habit.

The order I would suggest, for a decision rather than as a plan already made:

1. **The three headline items first**, because each is small, each is provably wrong, and
   none of them is a judgement call: transport, year-end plus branches, and the
   certificate route.
2. **Then one shared way of asking the table**, so a route says which screen it belongs to
   and the answer comes from `profile_matrix.py`. That is what removes the class of
   problem instead of 62 instances of it.
3. **Then the rest, screen by screen rather than route by route**, because the routes group
   naturally into the screens the table already names.

**Nothing here should widen anybody's access.** Every item above is a narrowing. The pinned
per-profile reach counts should not move at all when this work is done, and if one does, it
means somebody's access changed and nobody decided to.

**One thing this survey cannot tell you.** It proves who gets past the door. It does not
prove what is in the room. A read route that answers 200 against an empty stand-in database
may still filter what it returns on the live system. Where that matters, it is called out
above.
